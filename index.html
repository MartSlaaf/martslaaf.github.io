About

I have worked on neural networks since 2013 and published my first papers on them in 2015, on wavelet networks and on how to initialise multilayer networks. Since then I have tried to work on machine learning that people use: first in security and banking, then in X-ray imaging for physicists and biologists, and now in AI agents for software engineering.

At JetBrains Research I grew a research team from one person to nine. Since August 2026 I am Lead of ML Research and lead the team leads, about 36 people in total. Several people from the team now lead projects and directions of their own, and I count that among my best results.

I try to understand a problem as deeply as it needs: sometimes that means the physics of an imaging setup, sometimes a long chain of “why?” about an agent's behaviour. I also try to stop my own ideas early when the evidence is against them.

Current work

My teams study what makes software engineering agents work in practice: the environments they run in, how to train them, how they handle long context, and how to evaluate them.

Environments
EnvBench is a benchmark of 994 Python and JVM repositories that are hard to set up. PIPer trains Qwen3-8B with SFT and RL with verifiable rewards; it then sets up environments on par with Qwen3-32B and GPT-4o and runs on consumer hardware.
Context
The Complexity Trap shows that masking old observations halves an agent's cost and matches LLM summarisation in solve rate. A follow-up studies why implicit context compression fails on multi-step coding tasks.
Training
Step Rejection Fine-Tuning keeps unresolved agent trajectories and masks only the wrong steps: +3.7 pp on SWE-bench Verified, against +2.4 pp for plain rejection fine-tuning.
Evaluation
GitGoodBench covers Git workflows. Software world models test whether models predict the memory, time, and profiles of real code. For commit messages, we chose offline metrics by how well they match what users edit online.
Products
The team built an agent-platform prototype, used it in projects with product teams, and took it to production with two of its members; the production platform, rewritten for product needs, is now Koog. Our notebook error-resolution agent shipped in Datalore.
